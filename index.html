Prepare your records

Evidence is more useful than promises.

Good documentation can help an exchange, investigator, lawyer, or law-enforcement agency understand the incident. You do not need to share sensitive wallet credentials to explain what happened.

✓Transaction hash or transaction ID, if available.
✓Sending and receiving wallet addresses.
✓Network name, asset, amount, and approximate date.
✓Messages, website addresses, receipts, and payment records.
✓Exchange account records and relevant case numbers.

Before contacting anyone

  • Save original records and keep backup copies.
  • Do not edit screenshots in a way that changes the evidence.
  • Check transaction details on the appropriate blockchain explorer.
  • Contact your exchange or payment provider promptly when relevant.
  • Consider reporting suspected crime to the appropriate authorities.
  • Do not publish personal information or sensitive account details publicly.
✓

Protect your wallet first

Never disclose your seed phrase, private key, password, or one-time authentication code to someone claiming to help. Do not install remote-access software at a stranger's request.

Frequently asked questions

Know the risks before you act

Practical answers to common questions about cryptocurrency fraud and recovery claims.

Can stolen cryptocurrency always be recovered?

No. Recovery is never guaranteed. Blockchain transfers are generally irreversible, and the outcome depends on factors such as where the assets went, whether an exchange can identify or freeze them, the available evidence, and applicable law.

Can someone recover funds using only a transaction hash?

A transaction hash can help verify a transfer and trace its on-chain path. It does not grant control over the receiving wallet and does not, by itself, enable anyone to reverse a transaction.

Should I pay a fee to unlock my profits or recover my funds?

Be cautious of demands for taxes, deposits, verification payments, gas fees, or other charges sent to a private wallet before a promised release. Verify the claim independently through official channels. A demand for another payment is a common warning sign of fraud.

What if a recovery company guarantees success?

Treat guarantees with skepticism. Ask for the company's legal identity, qualifications, written scope of work, full fee terms, and independently verifiable references. Verify any claimed regulatory status with the relevant regulator.

What if my wallet or exchange account was compromised?

Use the provider's official website or application to secure your account, change affected passwords, revoke suspicious sessions or approvals where appropriate, and contact official support. If a seed phrase was exposed, consider moving remaining assets to a securely created wallet. Never enter the phrase into a website suggested by a stranger.

What information should I share for an initial discussion?

Start with a brief description, approximate date, asset and network, and whether you have a transaction hash. Avoid sending identity documents, full account records, or other sensitive information until you have independently verified who you are dealing with and why that information is necessary.

Does contacting this website guarantee an investigation or recovery?

No. Contacting this website does not guarantee that a case will be accepted, independently investigated, or resolved. Confirm the operator's identity, qualifications, scope of service, privacy practices, and fees before engaging any service.
